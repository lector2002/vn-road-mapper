# Global Flood Database in the flood-risk layer: the north

> 2026-11-22. Public data only: adds the Global Flood Database (Tellman et al. 2021, v1.4) to the Phase 5 layer. Same truth, roads and dev/test split as [Phase 5](2026-11-22-phase5-flood-risk.md). Nothing posted to or edited on OSM.

## Result

In the north (the dev floods: EMSR754 Red River delta, September 2024, and EMSR844 Quang Châu, October 2025), 1.6% of 98,155 roads flooded. `high` roads now flood 4.8% of the time instead of 2.7%, `high` catches 60% of the flooded roads instead of 28%, and the class AUC rises from 0.66 to 0.74. On the held-out central flood (EMSR848) `high` is more precise (45.9% flooded instead of 35.0%) with about the same reach (42% of flooded roads instead of 43%) and the same AUC (0.69).

"Before" is the Phase 5 layer rebuilt on the same roads: it reproduces the Phase 5 numbers exactly.

**The north is where the thresholds are fitted, so its gain is partly in-sample.** To check it out of sample, the thresholds were also fitted on EMSR848 and scored on the north: without GFD `high` is 3.4% flooded and catches 12% of flooded roads (AUC 0.66); with GFD 4.1% and 65% (AUC 0.72). GFD's own threshold comes out at 10% of the way either way.

## Why it helps in the north

The north had almost no earlier flood maps: 2,546 of the 98,155 dev roads (2.6%) touch an earlier UNOSAT or EMS map, and that layer ranks them at chance (AUC 0.51). The Global Flood Database marks 17,976 dev roads (18%) as flooded at least once in 2000–2018, and on its own ranks them at AUC 0.71. Those roads flooded 5.1% of the time, 3.2 times the base rate, and hold 57% of the flooded roads.

In central Vietnam it adds less: 12,416 test roads (7%) touch a GFD event, 4,153 of them already in an earlier UNOSAT map. Its single-layer AUC there is 0.60 against 0.68 for the UNOSAT/EMS history.

## What changed

- `pipeline/flood/risk.py`: `way_features(..., gfd=)` adds `gfd`, the share of a way's 25 m points flooded in any GFD event, and `gfd_events`, the most events at one point. `risk()` makes a way `high` when `gfd` reaches `rules["gfd"]`; rules without `gfd` behave as before.
- `tools/floodcheck.py`: `fetch` lists the public `gfd_v1_4` bucket, keeps the 76 single-file events whose map overlaps Vietnam (2000-07 to 2018-11, 1.3 GB) and downloads them. `build` counts, per 250 m pixel, the events that flooded it, leaving out GFD's permanent-water band and any event starting after the flood being scored. `fit` also picks the `gfd` threshold (0.1, 0.2, 0.5 or 1.0) on dev.
- Rules fitted on dev: `high` at 20% of the way in an earlier UNOSAT/EMS map, or 10% in a GFD event, or at 3 m or less and 3 m or more below its surroundings; `medium` at 3 m or less or water seen 10% of the time. Before: 5 m and 1 m below.
- `tools/province.py flood` adds the GFD layer and writes `gfd` and `gfd_events` to `flood_risk.csv`.

## Caveats

- **250 m pixels.** A road in a GFD pixel may have stayed dry while the paddy around it flooded. The layer marks "this area floods", not "this road floods".
- **2000–2018, MODIS, cloud-limited.** Floods under cloud or shorter than a few days are missed; anything since 2019 is not in it.
- **Precision in the north stays low.** Only 1.6% of northern roads flooded in these maps, so even `high` is dry 95% of the time. The class is a ranking, not a warning.
- **One test flood.** EMSR848 is still the only held-out event; the Mekong delta is untested.

## Reproduce

```bash
pip install -r requirements-flood.txt
python -m tools.floodcheck fetch output/flood        # now also the Global Flood Database events near Vietnam
python -m tools.floodcheck ways vietnam-latest.osm.pbf output/flood
python -m tools.floodcheck build output/flood
python -m tools.floodcheck score output/flood/flood_ways.csv   # the fit takes about 10 minutes
```
